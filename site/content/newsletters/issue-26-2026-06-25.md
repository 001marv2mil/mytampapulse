### 🌤️ Tampa's 7-Day Weather Whisper

Peak summer and the rainy season is fully operational. The National Weather Service has the week sitting in the low to mid 90s all seven days with a heat index running 100 to 107 degrees most afternoons. Overnight lows are staying in the upper 70s to 80 degrees, warm but workable once the sun is down. The afternoon storm window is the standard Tampa summer pattern. Get outdoor plans done before 2 PM or push everything after 7 PM. The evening window after storms clear is genuinely good this week, the Gulf water is as warm as it gets all year, and sunsets are hitting around 8:30. That two-hour window between storm clearing and full dark is the best free thing Tampa has going right now.

### 🎯 Marv's Tampa Pro Tips

- **Creative Loafing Meet the Chefs at The Vault**, tonight June 25. Fourteen chefs from the restaurants that are running Tampa's food scene right now are all in one room tonight. The lineup includes chefs from Rusty Pelican, O-Ku, Sal y Mar, Sunda New Asian, and Bulla Gastrobar. Tasting format, three hours, the kind of access to Tampa's kitchen talent that does not happen often. Tickets start at $85 at meetthechefstampabay.com. Check availability now because the room is not unlimited.
- **Godfrey at Funny Bone Ybor City**, June 26 to 28, 1600 E 8th Ave inside Centro Ybor. Godfrey has been one of the most consistent working comedians in the country for over a decade and the Funny Bone in Ybor is a proper room for stand-up. Friday shows run at 7 PM and 9:45 PM. Saturday at 6:30 PM and 9:15 PM. Sunday at 6:30 PM. Tickets from $32. This is not a name the Funny Bone books every week. Go this weekend.
- **Bosc Modern Bar + Kitchen**, 201 W Platt St, Hyde Park. Opened in February and Tampa has not fully caught up yet. The concept is premium steaks and a sushi bar under one roof, which sounds like too much, but the kitchen has a clear point of view on both sides and they do not bleed into each other. Parker Street Bakery is co-located in the same building. Book a reservation for a weeknight. The weekend tables are already going fast and the weeknight experience is better anyway.
- **Fusillo**, 905 Central Ave, St. Pete EDGE District. Opened in March. Fast-casual fresh pasta bowls from $13.50 to $15.50. The noodles are made properly, the portions are honest, and the EDGE District location means the whole neighborhood is available before or after without getting back in your car. This is the dining discovery of the week. The line is coming. Get there before it does.
- **Sunsets at Pier 60**, Pier 60 Park, Clearwater Beach, every night, free. This event has been happening continuously since 1995. Two hours before sunset to thirty minutes after. Craft and artisan vendors, street performers, and the Gulf sunset. If you have lived in Tampa Bay for any amount of time and have not been, the excuse window is closed. Go on a weeknight this week when parking is easy and the crowd is manageable.
- **Luma at SkyBeach Resort**, 6800 Sunshine Skyway Ln S, St. Pete. Chef Ryan VanDusen opened this Mediterranean concept in January and it still has not gotten mainstream Tampa attention. The location at SkyBeach Resort on the Sunshine Skyway approach is one of the most dramatically situated restaurants in the Bay area. Water views directly out at Tampa Bay, Mediterranean menu, local ingredients. Make a reservation. The weeknight availability is there right now and it will not stay that way once the food press catches up.

### 💎 Hidden Gems

- 🌿 **Sawgrass Lake Park in St. Pete**, off 62nd Ave N. This is one of the only maple swamp boardwalk experiences in Florida and almost nobody outside of birders knows it exists. The raised wooden boardwalk runs through a canopy of red maples, willows, and cypress directly over the water, with alligators visible below and anhinga drying their wings in the trees above. Free to enter. Open daily at 7 AM. Go early, bring binoculars, and give yourself at least ninety minutes. The trail connects to a full lake loop and the whole thing feels like something that belongs three states north of here.
- 🏖️ **Cypress Point Park**, 5620 W Cypress St, Tampa. Municipal park on Old Tampa Bay in the Westshore area that most people drive past without knowing it is there. There is a small beach on the bay, a swimming area that locals use without the Clearwater crowds, a boat launch, and a fishing pier with direct water views back toward downtown. The park is rarely crowded even on weekend mornings and the bay views from the picnic areas are genuinely excellent. Free to enter. Go on a weekday morning if you can.
- 🛶 **Cockroach Bay Preserve**, off Cockroach Bay Rd, Ruskin. Drive south of Tampa for thirty minutes and this place feels like another state. The preserve covers hundreds of acres of undeveloped mangrove estuary on Tampa Bay's eastern shore. A kayak launch puts you directly into some of the most pristine mangrove habitat remaining in the Bay area and the bird life is extraordinary. The name keeps most people away. That is exactly the point. Kayak rentals are available nearby. Go early on a calm morning when the water is flat.
- ☕ **Independent Roasters**, 4306 N Florida Ave, Seminole Heights. Neighborhood coffee shop in a restored bungalow on North Florida Avenue that the Seminole Heights crowd treats as a living room. The espresso program is serious, the sourcing is transparent, and the vibe is the opposite of a corporate coffee experience. No drive-through, no app, just a room full of regulars and good coffee. The back patio on a weekday morning is one of the better quiet hours available in the neighborhood.
- 🦋 **USF Botanical Garden**, University of South Florida campus, Tampa. Free and open to the public with almost no foot traffic outside of botany students. The collection covers over 6,000 plant specimens across a walking garden that includes a butterfly garden, a native Florida plant section, a carnivorous plant exhibit, and a koi pond. On a weekday morning the place is nearly empty. It is twelve acres of genuinely interesting horticulture sitting behind a university campus that most Tampa residents have never walked through. Free parking available on campus on weekends.
- 🍞 **La Segunda Central Bakery**, 2512 N 15th St, Ybor City. Every Cuban sandwich in Tampa gets made with La Segunda bread but almost nobody has actually been to the bakery. It has been standing in Ybor City since 1915 and it is still making the bread the same way it always has, in the same building, on the same street. Walk in, buy a loaf directly, and eat it on the way to your car. The bakery itself is not a restaurant but it is one of the most purely Tampa experiences you can have in this city. Open in the early morning hours. Go before 8 AM.
- 🍷 **Mise en Place**, 442 W Kennedy Blvd, South Tampa. Tampa had a serious fine dining scene before the Instagram era and Mise en Place is what it looked like. Chef Marty Blitz has been running this room since 1986 and the kitchen still operates at a level that most newer restaurants in the city have not reached. The interior is quiet, the service is unhurried, and the menu changes with what is actually good and available. Younger Tampa residents have mostly never heard of it. The people who have been going for twenty years are not in a hurry to share the table availability. Worth a reservation.
- 🌮 **Datz**, 2667 S MacDill Ave, South Tampa. The most creative comfort food menu in the city operated out of a South Tampa neighborhood spot that the tourists never find because it is on MacDill instead of downtown. The mac and cheese variations alone are worth the trip. The bar program is serious, the space is loud and fun, and the kitchen executes a menu that should not work as well as it does. Neighborhood crowd, no pretense, consistent execution. This is the kind of restaurant Tampa has more of than most cities and somehow keeps to itself.

### ⭐ Community Pick

> This week the spotlight goes to **Zack Wittman**, the Tampa photographer who is doing something that sounds simple and turns out to be urgent: documenting the last cigar factories still operating in Ybor City before they are gone.
>
> Ybor City was built on hand rolled cigars. At the industry's peak in the early 1900s, the neighborhood had over 200 factories and 12,000 cigar workers, making Tampa the cigar capital of the world. Today a handful of operations remain. Wittman is photographing them while they still exist, the workers, the rooms, the light coming through the factory windows, the specific quiet of a place where the same craft has been practiced for over a century. The subject is Tampa's actual history, not the curated version of it.
>
> Creative Loafing named Wittman one of seven emerging Tampa Bay artists to watch in 2026. The recognition is deserved but the work itself is the thing. Photography that documents living history carries a weight that gallery work alone rarely does, because the subject is real and the clock is running. When the last factories close, the only version of them that survives will be in photographs. Wittman is making sure those photographs exist.
>
> This city loses things faster than it records them. Wittman is working against that current. Follow his work and pay attention to what he puts out this year.
