Summer Solstice is this Saturday, Tampa. June 21st. The longest day of the year. Tampa gets 14 hours and 9 minutes of daylight and the Gulf is at its warmest point of the entire calendar. Most people will spend it at the same beaches and the same restaurants they always go to. This week we are going somewhere else. There is a restaurant on the Hillsborough River that brews its own beer and sits next to an actual natural spring that most Tampa residents have never seen. There is a beach that requires a ferry to reach and is almost always empty compared to the Clearwater crowds fifteen minutes away. Tampa Bay Rowdies are playing and most of you forgot the city has a soccer team. This week is about the Tampa that does not make the usual list. Let's get into it.

### 🎯 Marv's Tampa Pro Tips

- **Ulele on the Hillsborough River**, North Boulevard, Tampa Heights. This restaurant deserves to be talked about every single week until the reservation list is six months deep because it is that good and still that underappreciated. The concept is built around Florida and Indigenous culinary traditions, the beer program is brewed on site in a visible brewhouse, and the outdoor deck on the Hillsborough River is one of the best dining settings in the city. The natural Ulele Spring sits on the property. There is a reason the site was important to the Seminole people and there is a reason the restaurant built its identity around that history. Dinner on the deck during a Solstice week sunset is the move.
- **Tampa Theatre summer film series**, 711 Franklin Street, downtown Tampa. The Tampa Theatre opened in 1926 and the interior is one of the most extraordinary spaces in the city. The ceiling is painted like a Mediterranean night sky with fiber optic stars that actually twinkle. The auditorium has gargoyles, archways, and a working Wurlitzer pipe organ that gets played before certain screenings. The summer programming runs cult classics and revival screenings on weekend nights. Tickets are cheap, parking in the Franklin Street garage is easy, and sitting in a 1926 atmospheric movie palace watching a film on a summer night in Tampa is a completely different experience from a multiplex. Check their schedule this week.
- **Tampa Bay Rowdies at Al Lang Stadium**, St. Pete. The Rowdies are Tampa Bay's professional soccer team and they play in a stadium that sits literally on the water in downtown St. Pete. Al Lang has an open end that looks directly out at Tampa Bay. The club has been around since 1975 in various forms and has a genuine supporter culture that most Tampa sports fans have never experienced. Tickets are affordable, the tailgate scene around the stadium is good, and the view from the seats during a night match is unlike anything else in local sports. Check the Rowdies schedule for this week's home fixture.
- **Caladesi Island State Park** via ferry from Honeymoon Island. Most people who want a good beach go to Clearwater Beach or St. Pete Beach and deal with the crowds. Caladesi is eleven minutes away by ferry from Honeymoon Island and almost nobody is there. The beach is consistently ranked among the best in the country and it has no road access, which keeps it at a fraction of the crowd density of every other Gulf Coast beach in the area. Take the ferry, walk south from the dock, find a spot away from the boat drop area, and spend a few hours on one of the best stretches of sand in Florida with nobody around. The ferry schedule is posted on the Florida State Parks site. Get there early on weekends.
- **Ichicoro Ramen on North Florida Avenue** in Tampa Heights. Japanese-owned ramen shop that takes the broth seriously. The tonkotsu is cooked for 18 hours and the chicken shoyu is clean and precise. The space is small, the vibe is right, and this is not a place that cuts corners to move tables faster. Late night hours on weekends. One of those spots you walk past and then walk back and then end up at three times in one month.
- **Steelbach at Water Street Tampa**. The newest part of Tampa is still figuring out what it is and Steelbach is one of the places that gets it right. Smash burgers that take the format seriously, a cocktail program worth ordering off, and an interior that feels like a working steel foundry in a way that does not feel forced. The Water Street location means the surrounding block has filled in enough to make it a full evening rather than just a meal. This one is new enough that locals haven't caught up to it yet but that window is closing.
- **Weedon Island Preserve** in St. Pete, off Gandy Boulevard. Free to enter. Raised boardwalk through old growth mangrove canopy, a shell midden from the Weedon Island people who lived here over a thousand years ago, and more bird life than anywhere in the immediate metro area. The kayak launch is on site and rental outfitters operate nearby. This is real Florida in the middle of a metro area and almost nobody outside of birders and kayakers knows it exists. Go on a weekday morning, bring binoculars if you have them, and give yourself at least two hours.

### 📋 Digest

- ⚽ **The Tampa Bay Rowdies are a real professional soccer team with a real supporter culture** and a stadium that looks directly out at Tampa Bay. Most people in this city have never been to a match. This week is a good week to fix that.

### 💎 Hidden Gems

- 🗝️ **Eureka Springs Park in Tampa**. Hidden municipal botanical garden near Temple Terrace with a natural spring, peacocks that wander freely through the grounds, a cypress dome, and almost no foot traffic even on weekends. Most Tampa residents have lived here for years and never heard of it. Free to enter. Bring a camera. Go on a weekday morning and you might have the whole thing to yourself.
- 🛶 **Lettuce Lake Park on the Hillsborough River**. Free Hillsborough County park off Fletcher Avenue with a raised boardwalk through old growth cypress draped in Spanish moss. Alligators in the water below, great blue herons working the shallows, and the kind of old Florida cypress canopy that used to cover most of this region before development. Feels like a nature preserve three hundred miles north of here, ten minutes from USF.
- 🍜 **Sanwa Farmers Market off North Dale Mabry**. Japanese grocery and market that is almost entirely unknown outside of the Asian community in Tampa. Fresh fish counter, Japanese produce, housemade items. Genuinely unlike any other grocery experience in the city. The produce section alone is worth the trip.
- 🎭 **Stageworks Theatre in downtown Tampa**. Small professional theater company that produces genuinely ambitious work in an intimate 200-seat house. Not the Straz, not a touring production. Original work and local casts in a space where you are close enough to the stage to see the actors' faces clearly. Check their current production this week.
- 🌅 **Philippe Park in Safety Harbor**. Hillsborough County park on Old Tampa Bay with an ancient shell midden used by the Safety Harbor culture for over a thousand years, massive oak trees, and a fishing pier that looks back across the bay toward Tampa. The park is almost always quiet. The sunset view from the water's edge is one of the better ones in the entire Bay area. Twenty five minutes from downtown Tampa.

### ⭐ Community Pick

> This week the spotlight goes to **Jada Vidal**, the 23-year-old Tampa chef who took this city to national television and then came back here to keep cooking.
>
> Vidal grew up in Tampa Bay. She came up through local kitchens, earned a sous chef role at Noble Rice, and worked her way to Chef de Cuisine at Kinjo, one of Tampa's newer Italian-Japanese concepts in the Heights corridor. In fall 2025, she represented Florida on Season 24 of Hell's Kitchen, competing on a national stage while Gordon Ramsay evaluated every plate she put up. She earned her spot in that kitchen the same way she earned every step before it: by being better than the room expected.
>
> She came back. That part is the story. Tampa has a pattern of producing people who leave for bigger markets the moment they start getting good. Miami, Atlanta, New York — they all pull from this city's talent and most of it doesn't return. Vidal stayed. She is still cooking here, still building here, still choosing Tampa when the resume could take her anywhere.
>
> The food scene in this city is getting younger and more ambitious by the year. Jada Vidal is one of the people making that happen. Remember the name.
