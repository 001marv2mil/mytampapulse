### 🎯 Marv's Tampa Pro Tips

- **Juneteenth Tampa Bay Celebration at Curtis Hixon Waterfront Park**, Thursday June 19, all day and free. This is one of the most genuinely community centered events the city puts on all year. Live music running through the afternoon, local vendors, food, and the kind of crowd that shows up because they actually want to be there, not because they were looking for something to do. The Riverwalk setting right on the river makes it one of the best physical backdrops in Tampa. Get there in the morning before the heat peaks, find your spot under the trees, stay for the evening set when the park cools down and the music gets louder. If you have been meaning to make Juneteenth in Tampa a real thing on your calendar, this is the year.
- **Love Island Watch Party at Armature Works**, presented by @stufftodointampabay. Armature Works is the converted 1910 streetcar powerhouse on the Hillsborough River and it is already one of the best event venues in the city just standing still. When @stufftodointampabay fills it with a crowd locked in on Love Island, it becomes something completely different. These events sell tickets ahead of time so do not assume you can walk in the night of. Pull up their Instagram page, look for the pinned post, and get your ticket now. The vibe is going to be loud, social, and one of the more fun Thursday or Friday nights you will have this summer.
- **Anchor and Brines** is the Tampa dining spot that should be on your shortlist right now. The coastal concept is executed properly here, which is rarer than it sounds in a city that has water on three sides and still manages to serve mediocre seafood on half its menus. The menu is tight, every dish has a clear point of view, and the kitchen is not trying to cover too much ground. Go on a Tuesday or Wednesday night this week. The weekend wait is building and the weeknight experience is sharper anyway. This is a table you're going to tell people about.
- **Wandee Asian Fusion** keeps coming up in conversations about where to eat in Tampa and that is not an accident. The menu moves across Thai, Japanese, and broader Southeast Asian influences with a confidence that most fusion concepts never find. The flavors are clean and intentional, the portions are honest, and the space feels like the owners actually thought about the dining experience from the ground up. Book ahead if you're going on a Friday or Saturday. Weeknights you can usually walk in and get a spot at the bar, which is a perfectly good way to do it.
- **Rally Checkers truck** is earning its reputation in the Tampa food truck scene right now. The operation is focused and the execution is consistent, which is exactly what separates a food truck worth hunting down from one that just shows up in a parking lot. The schedule changes week to week so pull up their Instagram before you make a plan, because the truck moves and the location on Saturday is not always the same as Wednesday. Go on a weeknight if you can. The line is shorter and the food is just as good.
- **Tampa Blades** is on the calendar this week and this is the kind of local sports moment Tampa does not talk about nearly enough. The skill level is real, the atmosphere in the rink is different from anything else happening in the city this week, and the community that follows it is tight. Check their page for the exact time and location. If you have never gone, this week is as good a reason as any to show up for the first time.
- **St. Pete Night Market at Sundial**, Saturday June 21 starting at 5 PM. Monthly outdoor market in downtown St. Pete with local makers, food vendors, and live music running through the evening. The Sundial block is a good hang on its own and the market gives you a reason to commit to a St. Pete Saturday. Bridge traffic thins out after 4 PM, parking in the Central Avenue garages is easy once the afternoon clears, and you will have time to walk around Central before the market gets going. Worth the drive across the bridge.

That is not a trophy. If you walk anywhere in this city regularly you already know exactly why that ranking exists. Crosswalks that drivers ignore completely, sidewalks that vanish mid block, and roads that were designed around cars with zero consideration for anyone on foot. This is not a new problem and it is not getting better on its own. Worth knowing and worth being vocal about.
- 🌟 **Juneteenth programming is running across all of Hillsborough County on Thursday, not just at Curtis Hixon**. Check what's happening in your specific neighborhood because the county wide footprint is bigger than most people realize. The main stage is at Curtis Hixon but it is not the only stage.

### 💎 Hidden Gems

- 🗝️ **Westshore Saturday Farmers Market on Himes**. 9 AM to 1 PM and it is nothing like Hyde Park Village. No tourist traffic, no overpriced candles, no crowd of people who drove forty minutes to be seen at a farmers market. Actual Tampa farmers, actual produce, people who live within two miles of the thing. Get there before 10 if you want the good stuff. This is what a neighborhood farmers market is supposed to feel like.
- 🛍️ **Seminole Heights food crawl: Hair of the Dog, Ella's, Rooster and The Till, The Independent**. Three blocks, four completely different food vibes, zero tourist presence, and a neighborhood that actually has a point of view about what it is. Start at Hair of the Dog for the dive bar energy, Ella's for the eclectic brunch menu, Rooster and The Till for the farm to table plate that actually earns that label, and The Independent to close it out. Best three hour stretch you can do in Tampa without getting back in your car.
- 🏛️ **Oxford Exchange on West Kennedy Boulevard**. Historic building from the early 1900s, excellent coffee, bookshop in the back, full restaurant doing brunch and lunch properly. One of the most genuinely beautiful spaces in Tampa and it is always quieter than it should be. Bring someone you actually want to talk to. Street parking on Kennedy is easy before 11 AM.
- 🌿 **Julian B. Lane Riverfront Park after 6 PM on a weeknight**. Newer park on the Hillsborough River just west of downtown. Wide open lawn, direct river views, the bridge and city lights coming on as the sun drops. Almost nobody there Tuesday or Wednesday evenings. Bring food, bring a blanket, stay until the sky goes dark. Best free hour in the city right now.
- 🎨 **Tampa Museum of Art free Thursday evenings**. Open late and free every Thursday. Permanent collection is strong, building sits right on the river at Curtis Hixon, and this Thursday is Juneteenth which makes it a natural second stop after the Curtis Hixon celebration winds down in the afternoon. Walk right in.

### ⭐ Community Pick

> This week the spotlight goes to the team at **Wandee Asian Fusion**. Tampa has no shortage of restaurants that open loud and close quiet. Wandee did the opposite. They came in without a PR push, without a social media blitz, without the influencer dinner that every new restaurant does now as its opening move. They just opened the doors and cooked.
>
> The result is a dining room that fills up with people who found it the real way. Someone ate there, told someone they trust, that person went, and the cycle kept going. In a city where food and beverage concepts churn constantly and the next new thing gets a week of attention before the crowd moves on, building a loyal room through the quality of the food alone is genuinely difficult and genuinely impressive.
>
> Running an independent restaurant in Tampa in 2026 means you are competing against national chains with marketing budgets that would make your head spin, delivery platforms that take 30 percent off the top, and a dining public that has more options than it has ever had before. Wandee is winning that competition on merit. The menu is thoughtful, the hospitality is genuine, and the kitchen executes consistently. That combination is rarer than it sounds.
>
> If you have been meaning to go, stop meaning to and make the reservation. Support the places that are doing it right.
