### 🎯 Marv's Tampa Pro Tips

- **Bern's Steak House on South Howard Avenue** is the kind of Tampa institution that needs no introduction and somehow still does because half the people reading this have driven past it a hundred times and never gone in. It opened in 1953. Harry Waugh's Dessert Room upstairs has private booths built from wine casks. The wine cellar holds over half a million bottles, the largest restaurant wine collection in the world. The dry aged beef program is done on site. Dinner here is not a casual decision. It is an occasion. Reservations are available but they go. Call ahead, dress the part, and do not rush through it. This is one of those meals you will be talking about for a long time.
- **Columbia Restaurant in Ybor City** has been open since 1905 and it is still the most visually impressive dining room in Tampa. The flamenco show runs through the summer and the 1905 salad prepared tableside is the dish that has been on the menu longer than most families have lived in Florida. The summer menu update is worth a first visit or a return visit. Ybor parking is easier on weeknights. If you are going on a Saturday, get there before 7 or expect a wait. The Columbia does not take shortcuts on anything and it shows.
- **Summer Solstice is Saturday June 21**. Longest day of the year. Tampa gets 14 hours and 9 minutes of daylight on the Solstice and this weekend is the annual reminder that this city is best experienced outdoors when the conditions are right. The morning is clear, the Gulf is warm, the evening light runs long. Whatever you do this Saturday, do it outside. Bayshore at sunrise, a beach by noon, something on the water in the afternoon. The Solstice weekend is the peak of Tampa summer and it comes around once.
- **Fourth of July is ten days out** and if you are planning anything involving a restaurant reservation, a rooftop, a boat, or tickets to a fireworks event, the window is closing. Channelside, Sparkman Wharf, and the waterfront venues fill up weeks ahead. The Hillsborough River fireworks draw one of the largest crowds of the year downtown. If you are doing a house or backyard situation, the grocery stores will be out of the good stuff by the 3rd. Plan this week.
- **Tampa Bay Rays homestand at Tropicana Field** runs through the weekend. The Rays are in a real pennant race this summer and the June home games have energy that the early season didn't. Tropicana is air conditioned, the tickets are reasonably priced, and the baseball matters right now. Thursday and Friday night games are the best experience. Get there early, the pre-game happy hour at the venues around the Trop is worth the extra thirty minutes.
- **Gasparilla Distance Classic Summer Series at Flatwoods Park**, Saturday June 21 morning. Organized group run in one of Tampa's best green spaces. Flatwoods is the park on Bruce B. Downs that most people only drive past. The trail system loops through scrub pine and cypress and it is one of the better running environments in the region. Early start time so you are done before the Solstice heat peaks. Free parking, easy access from the interstate.
- **Channelside Summer Rooftop Series**, Saturday June 21 evening. Outdoor music and food vendors at the Channelside waterfront as the sun goes down on the longest day of the year. The Tampa skyline view from the Channelside rooftop spaces on a clear summer evening is one of the better free visuals in the city. Get there around 7, the sky does something interesting between 8 and sunset at 8:28.

### 📋 Digest

- 🔥 **Bern's Steak House wine cellar holds over half a million bottles and is considered one of the great restaurant wine collections in the world**. Most Tampa residents have never been. Most Tampa visitors put it on the list the moment they hear about it. That says something about how this city treats its own institutions.
- 🍽️ **Columbia Restaurant has been operating continuously since 1905**, making it the oldest restaurant in Florida. The Ybor location is the original. The flamenco show has been running for decades. If you have out of town guests this week, this is the reservation that requires zero explanation.
- 🌞 **The Summer Solstice on June 21 is the astronomical peak of summer**. Tampa gets more daylight on this day than any other. The Gulf water temperature is at its warmest point of the year. If there is a better time to be in this city, someone needs to make the case.
- 🎆 **Fourth of July events around Tampa Bay are booking up**. The Hillsborough River show downtown is one of the largest fireworks displays in the state. Waterfront restaurants and rooftops are taking reservations now. Do not wait on this one.
- ⚾ **The Rays are playing meaningful June baseball for the first time in a few years**. The pennant race conversation is real. Home games this week give Tampa a chance to actually watch a team that has a shot at something. Go in person if you can.
- 🌿 **Flatwoods Park in New Tampa is one of the most underused green spaces in Hillsborough County**. The trail system is 7 miles of paved loop through natural Florida scrub. Morning runs, bike rides, family walks. Free parking and almost no crowds on weekdays.
- 🏙️ **Water Street Tampa's summer programming is running through June and July**. The new district between downtown and the waterfront keeps adding events and activations. Worth a walk through if you have not been in a few weeks because it changes fast.

### 💎 Hidden Gems

- 🗝️ **Bayshore Boulevard at sunrise on a weekday**. The longest uninterrupted sidewalk in the United States runs four and a half miles along Hillsborough Bay. At 6 AM on a Tuesday it is quiet, the water is glass, the downtown skyline is lit up behind you, and the pelicans are working the shallows. One of the best free thirty minutes in Tampa. Bring coffee. Watch the sun come up over the bay.
- 🛶 **Canoe Escape on the Hillsborough River**. Outfitter on Fowler Avenue that rents canoes and kayaks for self guided paddles through the Hillsborough River Wilderness Preserve. Old Florida cypress canopy, turtles on every log, complete quiet. You can do a two hour trip or a full day. Feels nothing like you are twelve minutes from downtown Tampa. Go on a Tuesday morning and you will have the river almost entirely to yourself.
- 🏛️ **Henry B. Plant Museum on the University of Tampa campus**. The building is a Moorish Revival palace built in 1891 as a luxury resort by railroad magnate Henry Plant. Now a museum on the UT campus that most Tampa residents have never been inside. The architecture alone is worth the admission. The grand hall and the ballroom look like they belong in another country and another century. Weekday visits are quiet and unhurried.
- 🍸 **Cru Cellars on South Howard Avenue**. Wine bar two blocks from Bern's that does the opposite of everything the big steakhouse does. Small room, natural and small producer wines, no pretense, rotating selection written on a chalkboard. The kind of place you go when you want to try something you have never heard of and trust the people pouring it. Excellent cheese plate. Best seat in the house is at the bar talking to whoever is working.
- 🌊 **Beer Can Island on the Hillsborough River**. Unofficial sandbar at the mouth of the Hillsborough River where boaters tie up on weekends and weekday afternoons in the summer. If you know someone with a boat, this is the move. If you do not, the waterfront bars on Davis Islands have a direct sightline to the action. Tampa does not export this image of itself enough.

### ⭐ Community Pick

> This week the spotlight goes to the team at **Columbia Restaurant in Ybor City**. The restaurant opened in 1905. It has been operated by the same family, the Gonzmart family, for four generations. It has survived two world wars, prohibition, the collapse of the cigar industry that built Ybor City, urban decay, and every economic cycle Tampa has been through in the last 120 years.
>
> What makes that remarkable is not just the longevity. It is that the restaurant has not compromised to survive. The flamenco show still runs. The 1905 salad is still prepared tableside by a server who has done it ten thousand times. The Cuban bread still comes from La Segunda Central Bakery down the street, the same supplier they have used for decades. The dining room still looks like it was transplanted from Seville. They did not trade any of that away to stay relevant.
>
> Tampa has a complicated relationship with its own history. The city tends to tear down the old and replace it with something shiny. Columbia Restaurant is proof that you can honor what a place was and still thrive in what it is becoming. Four generations of a family betting on Ybor City when nobody else would.
>
> Go eat there this week. It is the most Tampa thing you can do with a Tuesday evening.
