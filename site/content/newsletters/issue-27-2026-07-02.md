Independence Day is tomorrow and it lands on the country's 250th birthday. Tampa Bay has four major July 4 celebrations spread across three cities, all of them free except Freedom Fest at the American Victory Ship on Channelside. The centerpiece is Liberty by the Bay at Julian B. Lane Park, a city-hosted event built around a 250-drone light show over the Hillsborough River at 9 PM. The show was designed specifically for America's semiquincentennial and nothing like it has been staged on the Hillsborough before. Clearwater opens its two-day festival at Coachman Park tonight, with what the city is calling the biggest fireworks display in Clearwater history tomorrow. The St. Pete Pier runs ten hours tomorrow with Uncle Sam's Market, live music, and their own fireworks show at 9 PM. The World Cup is in its knockout rounds and there is a free watch party this afternoon at the Sail Plaza outside the Convention Center for Argentina versus Cape Verde. The Midtown Tampa Sunset Market ran last night with forty-plus local vendors and no line. Two new spots have opened in the Bay area this summer that most people still have not found. Full breakdown below.

### 💡 Pro Tip

> *Liberty by the Bay is free and the 250-drone show over the Hillsborough at 9 PM is the kind of thing Tampa does not do every year. The best viewing spots are along the Riverwalk between the Cass Street Bridge and the Julian B. Lane Park entrance, facing south over the river. Get there by 6 PM before the Friends of the Riverwalk patriotic boat parade fills in the crowd behind you. The drone show runs directly above the water, so Riverwalk positions with an open view over the river are the ones you want. Parking is tight near the park. Use the Waterworks Park area or the Channel District and walk the Riverwalk north. Bring cash for the food vendors.*

The city built this year's celebration around America turning 250 and the headline is a 250-drone light show over the Hillsborough River at 9 PM, followed by a fireworks finale. The whole thing is free to enter. Get there early and stay on the Riverwalk side for the best drone show views.

- **Clearwater Celebrates America**, Coachman Park, 300 Cleveland St area, Clearwater. Tonight and tomorrow, free, 5 PM start each day. Tonight closes with a laser light show. Tomorrow closes with fireworks over Clearwater Harbor that the city is calling the biggest display in Clearwater history. Both days have vendors, food trucks, live music, and street performers. After tomorrow's fireworks, they are running a silent disco. Two free days on Clearwater's waterfront with the Gulf behind you.

- **World Cup Watch Party**, Sail Plaza at the Tampa Convention Center, today July 3. Free. 4 PM arrival, kickoff at 6 PM. The knockout stage is underway and today Argentina faces Cape Verde in the Round of 32. Food and drinks available for purchase at the plaza. If you have not watched any of the tournament yet, the elimination rounds are where the urgency shows up.

- **The Fourth at St. Pete Pier**, 600 2nd Ave NE, St. Pete. Tomorrow July 4, free, 4 to 10 PM. The pier runs Uncle Sam's Market with local vendors along the approach, food trucks, live music on the main stage, and fireworks at 9 PM. Free general admission. If you want reserved waterfront viewing, those tickets exist, but the free positions along the pier are plenty close to the water and the show.

- **Quiote Tequilaria**, 2109 Bayshore Blvd, Tampa. New this summer in the former Ciro's space on Bayshore. Chef Felicia Lacalle is running a fine dining modern Mexican kitchen with close to 175 tequila and mezcal options. The Tampa Bay Times reviewed it in June. The former Ciro's space sat in one of the best positions on Bayshore and the new kitchen is using it properly. Make a reservation. Weeknight tables are still available while Tampa catches up.

- **Easy Tiger Hi-Fi Lounge**, 915 First Ave N, St. Pete. Open since April in St. Pete's Edge District. Vinyl listening bar with hi-fi audio equipment and a full cocktail program. You sit down, order a drink, and listen to records through speakers worth listening on. Nothing else in the Edge District is doing this. It opened quietly in the spring and the city has not found it yet. That window is closing.

### 💎 Hidden Gems

- 🎣 **Ballast Point Park**, 5300 Interbay Blvd, South Tampa. Neighborhood fishing pier and park in one of Tampa's oldest residential areas that most people outside South Tampa have never visited. The pier extends out into Hillsborough Bay with open water views looking back toward downtown Tampa on one side and out toward open Tampa Bay on the other. Free to enter, free parking, fishing permitted. The surrounding Ballast Point neighborhood is one of the last parts of Tampa that looks the way it looked before the development wave hit. Go on a weekday morning when the only people there are locals.

- 🏖️ **Ben T. Davis Beach**, Courtney Campbell Causeway (FL-60), Tampa. Tampa's only public beach, sitting directly on the causeway between Tampa and Clearwater with Old Tampa Bay on both sides. Free parking. The water is calm and shallow because it is bay-side rather than Gulf-facing. The crowd is entirely local because tourists are driving past to reach Clearwater Beach. On a weekday morning the beach is nearly empty and the bay water in July is as warm as it gets all year.

- 🍽️ **Columbia Restaurant**, 2117 E 7th Ave, Ybor City. The oldest restaurant in Florida, open since 1905. The same family has been running it ever since. The building covers a full city block in Ybor and the Flamenco show still runs in the main dining room. The food is classic Cuban-Spanish, the paella is made to order for two or more, and the sangria recipe has not changed in decades. Younger Tampa residents often skip it because nothing about the exterior signals it is worth going in. It absolutely is. Go on a weeknight, get the Don Quijote room, order the paella.

- 🌿 **Boyd Hill Nature Preserve**, 1101 Country Club Way S, St. Pete. 245 acres of Florida ecosystems inside the St. Pete city limits that almost nobody outside the birding community knows about. The preserve covers six distinct habitat types, pine flatwoods, scrub, hardwood hammock, and two lakes. The bird list covers over 240 species. Three dollars for non-residents. No crowds. On a July morning when the rest of St. Pete is still recovering from the holiday weekend, the loop trail around Lake Maggiore is one of the quieter walks in the Bay area.

- 🦐 **Tarpon Springs Sponge Docks**, 735 Dodecanese Blvd, Tarpon Springs. Greek fishing village forty minutes north of Tampa with actual working sponge boats still coming in and out of the harbor. The docks have been operating since Greek sponge divers arrived in 1905. The main street is tourist-facing but the working boats behind the shops are the real thing. The waterfront bakeries make loukoumades, Greek honey fritters, that have not changed in a hundred years. Go early on a Saturday when the boats are coming in and the dockside scene is still before the afternoon crowd.

- 🛶 **Weedon Island Preserve**, 1800 Weedon Dr NE, St. Pete. 3,190 acres of protected wetlands and maritime hammock on Tampa Bay in St. Pete that does not show up on tourist lists. The boardwalk trail goes out over the mangroves directly onto the bay, and on a clear morning the bird activity is extraordinary. No entrance fee. The cultural center on site covers the Native American history of a site that has been inhabited for at least 10,000 years. It is the most archaeologically significant location in the Bay area and has a seven-car parking lot. Go on a weekday.
