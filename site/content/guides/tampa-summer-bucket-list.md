Summer here is not a season you push through. It is a season you schedule around. From May into September the day splits into a good morning, a hostile afternoon, and a second good evening. The people who enjoy summer in Tampa Bay moved everything into the first and third. Here is the list worth finishing before October.

## Learn The Two Clocks

Before 1 PM you get flat water, open parking lots, and heat you can still negotiate with. After 7 PM you get the second shift: cooling asphalt, sunset light, and outdoor tables that are finally usable. The middle is not yours. Florida's peninsula is narrow enough that the Gulf sea breeze and the Atlantic sea breeze collide inland most afternoons, and the corridor between Tampa and Orlando gets more cloud to ground lightning than anywhere else in the country. Storms fire mid to late afternoon, dump hard for 30 to 60 minutes, then clear into a good evening.

So: out the door by 7:30 AM for anything on the water, back in the car by 1 PM, afternoon indoors, back out at 7. Carry more water than feels reasonable, park facing east so the car is shaded later, and when you hear thunder, get off the water.

## Springs Worth The Drive

Spring water sits near 72 degrees year round, which in August is painful for 45 seconds and then perfect. All of them fill up.

- **Weeki Wachee Springs State Park**, 6131 Commercial Way, Spring Hill. Admission is $13 for adults and $8 for kids 6 to 12. The park regularly hits capacity and closes the gate for the rest of the day, often before noon on a summer weekend, so treat 9 AM as late. Buccaneer Bay is the only spring fed water park in the state. Paddling requires a reservation even with your own boat, and the river down to Rogers Park at 7244 Shoal Line Blvd is a protection zone: no beaching, anchoring, or standing on the banks. Rangers write tickets.
- **Three Sisters Springs**, Crystal River. The clearest water within two hours of Tampa. The rule: no water access from land. Walk the boardwalk from the shuttle, or swim and paddle in, but you cannot combine them. Paddle craft are allowed inside the springs roughly April 1 through November 14. Launch from Hunter Springs Park, 104 NE 1st Ave, at first light. By 10 AM the run is a traffic jam of rental kayaks.
- **Rainbow River**, Dunnellon. KP Hole Park at 9435 SW 190th Avenue Road sells a tubing package around $30 covering entry, tube, and the shuttle back, with rentals cut off at 12:30 PM or whenever the park fills. The float runs about four hours. The state park tubing entrance at 10830 SW 180th Ave Rd does a tube and tram ticket near $25, 8 AM to 4 PM daily in summer.
- **Lithia Springs Conservation Park**, 3932 Lithia Springs Rd, Lithia. The closest real spring to Tampa, roughly 40 minutes out. Summer hours 8 AM to 7 PM, $2 per vehicle for up to eight people, $2 per person to swim. Swim bands sell on site only, never by phone, in two sessions capped at 200 swimmers each: 9 AM to 1 PM and 2 PM to 6 PM. Take the morning one. The afternoon session is the one you spend watching lightning from the parking lot.
- **Hillsborough River State Park**, 15402 US-301, Thonotosassa. Six dollars per vehicle for up to eight people, $4 if you are alone, 8 AM to sundown. The Class II rapids are the curiosity here, whitewater in a state with almost none, best seen from the suspension bridge. The park pool has been closed since 2022, so plan on the river instead, and canoe and kayak rentals are available at the concession building.

## Beaches Locals Actually Use

- **Ben T. Davis Beach**, 7740 W Courtney Campbell Causeway. The one you hit on a Tuesday after work because it is 15 minutes from downtown. Metered parking around $2.50 an hour, sunrise to sunset. Not the best sand, easily the most convenient.
- **Cypress Point Park**, 5620 W Cypress St. Quieter, with free parking Monday through Friday until noon, which is the whole trick. Shaded tables, a disc golf course, and a paddleboard launch. Dogs are welcome on the trails but not on the sand.
- **Fort De Soto North Beach**, 3500 Pinellas Bayway S, Tierra Verde. Still the best public beach in the region, back open after the hurricane closures. Parking is $6, gates open at 7 AM, and there are small tolls on the Pinellas Bayway coming in. The lagoon on the north end is the calmest water for kids anywhere close. Arrive by 9 AM on a weekend.
- **Caladesi Island**, by ferry from Honeymoon Island State Park, 1 Causeway Blvd, Dunedin. Parking at Honeymoon is about $8, the ferry roughly $20 round trip for adults and $10 for kids 6 to 12, sold in person only at the ticket office. Boats start at 10 AM, run about every half hour in summer, and cap your stay at four hours. Take the first one.

Skip Clearwater Beach between 11 AM and 4 PM in July. The sand is fine. The causeway is not.

## Get On The Water Early

- **Weedon Island Preserve**, 1800 Weedon Dr NE, St. Petersburg. Free, open 7 AM until 15 minutes before sunset. The south paddling trail is a marked four mile loop through mangrove tunnels, about three hours. Check the tide, because at low water the tunnels get shallow enough to drag. Launch at the end of Weedon Dr NE by the pier.
- **Cockroach Bay**, boat ramp at 5299 Cockroach Bay Rd, Ruskin. Free launch, open sunrise to sunset, one portable restroom. Two marked trails, the Snook Trail at roughly two miles and the Horseshoe Crab Trail at roughly five, through some of the least developed shoreline left on Tampa Bay. For people who found Weedon Island too busy.
- **Ballast Point Pier**, 5300 Interbay Blvd. Free and open around the clock even though the park closes at 8 PM. Fishing needs a Florida saltwater license. Best cheap sunset in South Tampa.

## Eat Like It Is July

- **West Tampa Sandwich Shop**, 3904 N Armenia Ave. Open 6 AM to 3 PM Monday through Saturday, closed Sunday. Order the honey Cuban, and go before 11 AM or wait behind everyone from the courthouse.
- **La Segunda Bakery**, the original in Ybor City on North 15th Street and the cafe at 4015 W Kennedy Blvd. Baking Cuban bread since 1915, and most Cuban sandwiches in town start with their loaves. Buy it in the morning while it is warm.
- **Mango season** runs May into October. Tommy Atkins arrive in May and June, Hadens in June and July, Keitts in August and September. Sanwa Farmers Market at 2621 E Hillsborough Ave opens absurdly early and sells cases at prices no grocery store touches. Better still, find a neighbor in Seminole Heights with a loaded tree and offer to pick.
- **Raw bars** are the correct summer dinner. Oystercatchers at 2900 Bayport Dr does it on the water at the Grand Hyatt. Ted Peters at 1350 Pasadena Ave S has smoked mullet over red oak at that corner since 1951, and the fish spread with saltines is the point. Bring cash, and note they are closed Monday and Tuesday.

## The Evening Shift

- **Sunsets at Pier 60**, 1 Causeway Blvd, Clearwater Beach. Free nightly, from two hours before sunset to two hours after, with buskers and craft tables. Park in a garage several blocks back and walk in.
- **Rock the Park**, Curtis Hixon Waterfront Park, 600 N Ashley Dr. Free live music the first Thursday of each month, usually around 6:30 PM. Bring a blanket and walk in from Water Street.
- **St. Pete Shuffle**, 559 Mirror Lake Dr N. Fridays 7 to 10 PM at the oldest shuffleboard club in the world. Free for members, around $10 otherwise, kids free. Volunteers teach you the game in four minutes.
- **The Bayshore seawall** after 7:30 PM. Four and a half miles of sidewalk, pleasant only once the sun is off it. Park on a side street near Bay to Bay and walk north.

## Storm Afternoon Escapes

When the radar goes purple around 3 PM, these are the moves.

- **Tampa Museum of Art**, 120 W Gasparilla Plaza. Art on the House runs Thursdays 4 to 8 PM, pay what you will. The best free air conditioning downtown.
- **The Florida Aquarium**, 701 Channelside Dr. Tickets are priced by date and usually land somewhere in the $30s for adults. Buying online ahead of your visit gets the lowest price.
- **Tampa Theatre**, 711 N Franklin St. A 1926 movie palace running current films and classics in serious cold. Sit in the balcony.
- **Oxford Exchange**, 420 W Kennedy Blvd. Coffee, books, and a glass roofed room to wait out a downpour.

Get through half of this before Labor Day and you will have had a better summer than most people who live here. The trick was never heat tolerance. It was timing.
