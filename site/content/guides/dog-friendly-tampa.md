Tampa is an easy city to own a dog in, right up until August, when the pavement will burn a paw pad in under a minute and a toad in your backyard can kill a beagle in fifteen. Here is where dogs are genuinely welcome, when to go, and the Florida stuff nobody warns transplants about.

## The Dog Beaches

Three real ones in the region, and they are not interchangeable.

- **Davis Islands Dog Beach** (864 Severn Ave, Davis Islands 33606). Two fenced areas, one a standard dog park and one a small sandy beach where dogs go off leash into the bay. Roughly 7 AM to 7 PM, free, free parking. Go on a weekday morning. Weekend afternoons it becomes forty dogs on a strip of sand the size of a tennis court, and that is where fights start. Use the rinse station by the gate. Drive past Peter O. Knight Airport and keep going.
- **Fort De Soto Paw Playground and dog beach** (Fort De Soto Park, Tierra Verde). The best of the three by a wide margin. Two large fenced off leash areas with dog showers plus a real stretch of off leash beach on the water. Budget for the Pinellas Bayway toll and county parking fee. Park in the Gulf Pier lot, the dog areas sit just before the fort. It fills by mid morning on weekends.
- **Honeymoon Island pet beach** (1 Causeway Blvd, Dunedin). Entry is per vehicle, cheaper if you are alone in the car. The pet beach is at the south end, about a quarter mile walk from the lot, leashed only. Rangers enforce the six foot handheld leash rule and retractables are banned. Dogs are fine on the nature trails but not on Main Beach or North Beach.

## Dog Parks Worth The Drive

- **Jim Urbanski Dog Park at Al Lopez Park** (4810 N Himes Ave). Roughly 6:30 AM to 7 PM. Separate large and small sides, dog fountains, and real shade from mature oaks, the reason to pick it over a newer park.
- **Curtis Hixon Waterfront Park dog run** (600 N Ashley Dr). Small, fenced, turf, agility pieces, skyline behind it. A fifteen minute burn off before dinner. Turf holds heat, so skip it midday.
- **Queenie's Dog Park at Water Works Park** (1710 N Highland Ave). Best located park in the city. A minute from Ulele, five from Armature Works, directly on the Riverwalk.
- **West Dog Park** (6402 N Occident St) and **Northwest County Dog Park** (8951 W Waters Ave). Bigger, plainer, less crowded. The pick if your dog is reactive.
- **Hair of the Dog Park** (4211 N Nebraska Ave, Seminole Heights). A private off leash yard with a full bar in the middle. Day passes and memberships, and they check vaccination records, so send proof ahead of your first visit.

## Patios Where Your Dog Is Actually Welcome

Florida keeps dogs out of indoor dining rooms unless they are service animals, so everything here means the patio.

- **Magnanimous Brewing** (6809 N Nebraska Ave, Seminole Heights). Big open yard, rotating food truck, dogs everywhere. Weekday evenings are calm, Saturday afternoon is a scrum.
- **Armature Works** (1910 N Ola Ave, Tampa Heights). Dogs are welcome on the patio, courtyard and lawn but not inside the market hall. Settle on the lawn side, send one person in to order, eat outside facing the river.
- **Ulele** (1810 N Highland Ave). Sit down riverfront food with a leashed dog on the patio, and they keep a frozen treat for dogs. Request the patio when you book.
- **Hyde Park Village** (1602 W Snow Ave). The most dog forward few blocks in Tampa. Every outdoor common area is open to leashed dogs, several shops let them inside, and water bowls sit out along the sidewalks. Bartaco, Goody Goody, Forbici and On Swann all seat dogs outside. Use the free garage instead of circling for surface spots.
- **Gaspar's Grotto** (1805 E 7th Ave, Ybor). Loud, pirate themed, reliably fine with dogs on the front patio. Ybor's drawback is broken glass on 7th Avenue after weekend nights, so keep to daytime.
- **St. Pete**: **Pinellas Ale Works** (1962 1st Ave S) is about as dog centric as a brewery gets, and **3 Daughters Brewing** (222 22nd St S) has room to spread out. Patios along Central Avenue bring a bowl without being asked.

## Trails, The Riverwalk And Long Walks

- **Tampa Riverwalk**. Roughly two and a half miles of waterfront path from Channelside to Water Works Park, leashed dogs the whole way. Go before 9 AM or after sunset. There is almost no shade between Curtis Hixon and the convention center.
- **Bayshore Boulevard**. The long uninterrupted sidewalk along Hillsborough Bay. Completely exposed, and the concrete holds heat into the evening. A winter walk or a 6:30 AM walk.
- **Flatwoods Park** (14302 Morris Bridge Rd, Thonotosassa). A seven mile paved loop through pine flatwoods, mostly shaded, small entry fee per vehicle. Best long walk in the county.
- **Lettuce Lake Park** (6920 E Fletcher Ave). Leashed dogs are allowed on the trails but not the boardwalk, which is the part everyone drives out for. Know that before you pay at the gate.
- **Upper Tampa Bay Trail**. Paved and flat, easy in pieces from the trailheads off Ehrlich Road.

## Markets And Events

- **Ybor City Saturday Market** (Centennial Park, 1800 E 8th Ave). Saturdays year round, roughly 9 AM to 3 PM, free, pets welcome. Arrive by 10 AM in summer and use the Centro Ybor garage.
- **Hyde Park Village Fresh Market**, first Sunday of the month, dogs welcome throughout.
- Rescues and breweries run yappy hours and adoption events constantly, and the Humane Society of Tampa Bay calendar is the most reliable place to find them.
- Not every market allows dogs. The big St. Pete Saturday market has historically limited pets, so check that one first.

## Florida Hazards That Actually Matter

- **Heat and pavement**. From May through September, walk before 8 AM or after 8 PM. Press the back of your hand to the asphalt for seven seconds. If you cannot hold it there, your dog cannot walk on it. Flat faced breeds should not be out at midday in summer at all.
- **Bufo toads**, also called cane toads. Six to nine inches, active at night and after rain from June through October. Their milky toxin causes drooling, bright red gums, staggering and seizures, and it can kill a dog fast. If your dog mouths one, wipe the gums with a wet cloth and rinse the mouth sideways with a hose so water runs out and not down the throat, then drive to an emergency vet. Do not let the dog out unsupervised at night in the rainy season.
- **Alligators**. Assume every pond, canal, retention basin and stretch of the Hillsborough River holds one. Keep the dog leashed and ten feet back from any fresh water edge, and never let a dog swim in fresh water here. Dawn and dusk are the worst windows.
- **Red tide**. Brevetoxins go airborne during a bloom and dogs cough and wheeze worse than people do. Check the state wildlife commission daily sample map before any Gulf beach trip. If people on the sand are coughing, turn around, and never let a dog eat a dead fish.
- **Salt water**. Dogs that chase balls into the Gulf swallow a lot of it. Offer fresh water constantly and watch for vomiting on the drive home.

## Vet And Emergency Basics

- **BluePearl Tampa** (3000 Busch Lake Blvd, near Busch Boulevard and Dale Mabry) is open 24 hours, no referral needed for emergencies. Phone 813.933.8944. Put it in your contacts now, not while your dog is seizing in the back seat.
- **ASPCA Animal Poison Control**, 888.426.4435, staffed around the clock. There is a consultation fee. Call while you drive.
- **Humane Society of Tampa Bay** (3607 N Armenia Ave) runs low cost vaccine clinics, which is where to get the rabies certificate dog bars and boarding places ask for.
- Hillsborough County requires rabies vaccination and a license tag. Keep the tag on the collar, because it is the fastest way a neighbor gets your dog back to you.

Two rules cover most of it: go early, and carry more water than seems reasonable. The rest is knowing which gate to park at.
